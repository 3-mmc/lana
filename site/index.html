The demo runs Praat in this page and replays what real models returned for two readings of one sentence, in German and Georgian.

Phones from the signal

The phone tier is read off the acoustics by an IPA recogniser, with no pronunciation dictionary in the way. The word tier comes from a separate model.

So ანტარქტიკის comes out as ɑ n tʼ ɑ r kʼ tʼ i kʼ i s, ejectives and all, and the method works for languages that have no dictionary.

Praat's own measurements with every setting in view

Pitch, formants, intensity and the spectrogram are Praat's own, compiled to WebAssembly. Every argument is a field you can edit, and the page prints the commands it ran.

Tiers fill as it runs

Each tier has its own model, and results appear while the job is still going. A lane that is still being computed looks different from one that is empty.

Flexible for your workflow

Add as many tiers as a project needs: notes, glosses, comments, or a copy of any tier's boundaries to annotate the same stretches again.

The phone tier converts to ARPABET in CMU or TIMIT symbols. A sound ARPABET cannot write is kept and flagged for you to decide, never guessed.

To redo part of a recording, select it and run a model on that stretch alone. The rest of the tier stays as it was, hand corrections included.

Who is speaking

A speaker tier keys on the voice, not the words, so it needs no language code and runs alongside the recogniser.

Here the speaker changes from S1 to S2 at 6.04 s, and the silence between them is left unassigned.

Offline analysis completely on your computer without compromising performance

You stay in control of your data. The built-in models run on your own processor and work without a connection once downloaded, so audio, transcriptions and annotations never leave your computer.

That is what an ethics board or a data-management plan needs to hear about recordings of identifiable speakers.

It is quick too. The Dargwa sentence below took under six seconds on a laptop processor, with words from MMS and phones from ZIPA.

Lana itself connects to two places: Hugging Face when you download a model, and GitHub to check for a newer version. Neither request carries anything about your recordings.

Latest models made easy for linguists

Whisper, Meta's MMS and Omnilingual, ZIPA for IPA and a speaker model, sorted by the tier they fill. Each says what it is good at, how large it is and its licence before anything downloads.

MMS covers more than 1,150 languages, added one small download at a time. Each tier's menu offers only the models made for it.

Three ways to run it

Every version supports hand annotation, TextGrid import from Praat or ELAN, and recording from a microphone. Annotations save as you work.

Built-in recognition

At least one model per tier runs on an ordinary laptop. Which word model to use depends more on the language than on model size. MMS loads an adapter for each language, so it gets the script right where Omnilingual has to guess.
